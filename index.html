Your body hasn’t gotten the message that it’s over.

Even when you know it is.

You function. That has never been the problem. You are the one who handles it, who gets asked because you always say yes.

It is everything around the edges. Thoughts that arrive uninvited and will not leave. Lying awake exhausted. A feeling that something is off that you cannot point to or explain to anyone. The sense that you are built differently from everyone around you, and that if you described it properly nobody would know what you meant.

Maybe it feels like you have the map in hand but you still don’t quite know how to reach the destination.

That is not weakness and it is not a failure of effort. It is a nervous system that learned to stay switched on, because staying switched on worked.

You will do the hard thing. The physical thing, the long shift, the task nobody else wants. It is the four minutes of sitting still that take you apart, and almost everyone says so in the same words: I can’t do the slow stuff. I hate the part at the end where you just lie there.

Nervous system work, delivered as practice rather than instruction.

You learn what your own system is doing, in your own body, while it is happening. The practices are built to be self-verifying. The evidence shows up in your body, so you never have to take anyone’s word for it.

Every session is built around choice and pacing. Nobody is put on the spot, nobody is asked to disclose anything, and you can opt out of anything at any point without explaining yourself. You can take part seated, in a chair, on the floor, or standing, eyes open or closed, whatever lets you stay present.

What changes a nervous system is repetition, not intensity. Yoga in the West has come to mean the postures, which are one limb of eight. The others are breath, attention, rest, ethics, and the turning inward that the postures were only ever meant to prepare you for. This work draws on the whole of it, because that is where the rewiring happens, and because movement on its own was never the point.
